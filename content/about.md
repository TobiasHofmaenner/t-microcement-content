# Where this comes from

T-Microcement started in a renovation in **Medewi, West Bali**.

A working architect wanted a seamless, modern, high-quality finish
for her own villa. She tested the microcement systems on the local
market: the ones that looked right cost a fortune for a whole house,
and the affordable ones didn't perform. The gap between *good enough*
and *affordable* was too wide to live with.

So her partner — chemistry-curious, not chemistry-trained — said
"how hard can it be?" and started experimenting.

## Months of iterating

A dozen components, lots of prototypes, a series of test patches on
the villa, and a lot of *"this works in our test, but local
contractors can't repeat it."*

The hard part turned out not to be the finished properties. It was
making the system **workable by people who hadn't seen it before**,
**hard to mess up**, and **built from ingredients you can actually
source in Indonesia**.

The final formulation is a polymer-modified, metakaolin-densified
white cement with mineral pigments, titanium-dioxide for cool-touch
and UV stability, a small dose of zinc oxide for ongoing mould
resistance, and a citric-acid retarder so it stays workable in
tropical heat. The sealer is silicone-based, applied breathable on
purpose — the zinc has to keep migrating to the surface to do its
job. More on
[what's in it](/product/#whats-in-it) over on the product page.

## A year in our own home

The original villa was finished and has been lived in for a year.
Walls, floors, indoor and covered outdoor surfaces — every photo in
[the gallery](/gallery/) is real, no renders, no stock. No cracking,
no discolouration, no mould, normal wear on the floors that
sand-and-recoat handles in an afternoon.

We don't have lab certificates yet. We have a year of daily use
on a real building, and we publish what we know.

## Why we're selling it now

Visitors saw the finish. They asked where to get it. Several wanted
some for their own projects. After enough of those conversations, we
decided to make the product available — but we want to grow into it
slowly and honestly, not market our way into reputational debt.

So we're explicit about where we are: a **young product** with one
detailed reference project, not a certified industrial coating. We're
selling to early adopters who like the look, trust the chemistry, and
accept that we're still learning what the material does outside of
our own environment.

In exchange: **the bucket price you pay in year one is the price you
keep paying**, as long as you keep buying. No annual creep, no
loyalty-programme small print. As we accumulate more reference work
and the product matures, prices will move — but not for the people
who were here first.
