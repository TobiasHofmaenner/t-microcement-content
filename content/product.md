# The product

T-Microcement is a polymer-modified cement finish, applied 2–3 mm
thin, over your existing walls, floors, bathrooms, or furniture.

We sell the material. You — or your contractor — apply it. We're not
an applicator service, though we're happy to talk through unusual
cases before you order.

## The 3-layer system

The system is three separate products. Most projects order all three.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Primer** | Bonds the system to whatever you're coating — concrete, plaster, tile, plywood. The right primer is what lets the cement go directly over existing substrates without demolition. |
| **Main mix** | The look layer. Trowelled by hand in two thin passes. Available as a **wall mix** (smoother, no aggregate) or a **floor mix** (sand-loaded, rougher, tougher under traffic). |
| **Sealer** | A breathable silicone-based topcoat. Locks in water resistance without sealing the surface hermetically — there's a reason for that ([below](#the-sealer-is-breathable-on-purpose)). |

## What's in it

A dozen components, no marketing-flavoured "secret formula." We
don't publish the exact recipe (it's ours), but we'll tell you what
every ingredient does:

- **White Portland cement** — the binder. White, not grey, so colours come out clean.
- **Metakaolin** — a calcined-clay pozzolan. Locks the cement's free lime into the matrix, which makes the surface denser, less permeable, and largely immune to *efflorescence* (the white salty blooming you sometimes see on cement-based surfaces in humid climates).
- **Silica flour** — fine mineral filler for strength.
- **Titanium dioxide** — pigment and UV reflector. Brightens the natural off-white *and* keeps the surface cool-touch in direct sun.
- **Zinc oxide** — small dose of slow-release mineral. Inhibits mould and mildew long-term, which is why we deliberately *don't* hermetically seal the surface (the zinc has to migrate up to do its job).
- **SBR latex** — polymer modification. Gives the cured film some elasticity so it handles normal building movement without cracking.
- **Mineral pigments** — for colour. Default warm off-white; custom shades on request.
- **HPMC + PCE + polysiloxane** — workability admixtures. Make the mix trowel smoothly on vertical surfaces, stay workable in tropical heat, and not sag.
- **Citric acid retarder** — keeps the mix from setting too fast at 30 °C+ ambient.

Everything except the SBR latex is mineral. The system is **100% UV
stable** — it doesn't yellow or fade like resin-based "microcements"
(some of which are really coloured epoxy with sand).

## Where it works

- **Walls** — accent walls, full rooms, indoor or covered outdoor.
- **Floors** — residential and light commercial. 3 mm thin, no thresholds, no level changes.
- **Bathrooms** — walls, floors, shower areas. Waterproof with the sealer applied correctly.
- **Furniture & cabinetry** — built-ins, vanities, custom pieces.
- **Countertops & tables** — see the sealer note below.

## The sealer is breathable on purpose

Most microcements seal the surface hermetically with an acrylic or
epoxy topcoat. Ours uses a polysiloxane (silicone) network applied
breathable. Two reasons:

1. **The zinc has to keep working.** The zinc oxide mineralised into the mix needs to be able to migrate to the surface as the system ages. A hermetic seal would lock it inside and the long-term mould resistance would go away.
2. **It keeps the natural-stone feel.** Epoxy-sealed surfaces have a slight plastic quality; the breathable sealer leaves cement feeling like cement.

The trade-off: the surface is **waterproof but not fully stain-proof.**
Coffee, red wine, or cooking oil sitting on a flat horizontal surface
can leave a mark — easy to deal with on a floor (sand it out,
re-seal), worth thinking about for counters or tables.

**For counters or kitchen tables** that need to be properly impervious
to stains, plan on a **two-part epoxy topcoat** over our sealer. We
don't supply the epoxy; any quality counter epoxy works.

## Repair-friendly

One thing typical microcements are notoriously *bad* at is patch
repair — once a system has been on the wall for a year, blending in a
new patch is hard. Ours isn't, because the look is recoatable:

- **Stain or surface mark** → sandpaper it back, apply a thin re-seal.
- **Scratch or chip** → trowel a thin layer over the area, blend the edges, re-seal. The new layer bonds chemically to the old.
- **Whole-room refresh after a few years** → light sand, re-seal, looks new.

A small leftover bucket and a sanding sponge cover most of what
you'll need. We recommend holding back a bucket from every order.

## On durability — being straight with you

This is a custom-developed formulation. We have **not** put it
through formal laboratory testing, so we don't have certified
performance values to quote. We chose to be transparent about that
rather than borrow numbers from a generic spec sheet.

What we can tell you, from a year of using it in our own home:

- **Abrasion** — handles daily foot traffic well in residential use, with the sealer doing its job.
- **Impact** — polymer-modified, so it has some give. Still a thin cementitious layer — don't drop a hammer on it.
- **Water** — base material is dense (the metakaolin + silica flour combination does most of the work); the sealer is what makes the system fully waterproof. Don't skip the sealer.
- **Biology** — no mould, no growth in indoor tropical conditions over the year we've tracked.
- **UV** — no yellowing, no fading. Mineral system through and through.
- **Adhesion** — strong, no delamination, *as long as the substrate is properly prepared*.
- **Efflorescence** — the metakaolin locks free lime into the matrix, which is the main mechanism behind efflorescence. We haven't seen any in our reference project.

The honest disclaimer: because it's a thin-layer system, the
substrate underneath matters as much as the coating itself. A weak or
moving surface will telegraph through. Site prep isn't optional.

We describe it as a **durable, high-quality microcement system for
residential applications** with real-world performance we can show
you. It is **not a certified industrial product**, so for
commercial or high-stress applications, evaluate per project — and
we'll tell you honestly whether your case is one we'd take on.
